## Relation To The PPV Article

The current article
[`article/ppv_submission_article.md`](../article/ppv_submission_article.md)
measures two long-running public conference venues: the Zograf Readings and
the Roerich Readings. The Sementsov Readings are not part of that measured
two-venue corpus. They begin in 2022, are too recent for the present
long-duration comparison, and have a different institutional setting,
geography, and publication ecology.

The Paribok text should therefore be treated as contextual and interpretive
evidence, not as an additional row source for the Zograf/Roerich counts.

## Scope Decision

The current PPV article should not be expanded from two venues to three.
Its object is the main long-running observable conference infrastructure of
Russian Indology. Zograf and Roerich are selected because they are older,
stable, recurrent, and comparable public venues. Sementsov is a newer and
more marginal conference for the purposes of this article; it is useful as
context, but it is not yet a comparable main venue.

Including Sementsov would weaken the article's design by mixing the two main
long-running venues with a recent peripheral initiative. The article should
therefore remain a Zograf/Roerich study.

## What The PDF Supports

- A newer marginal contextual venue, the Sementsov Readings, exists beside the
  older Zograf and Roerich conference ecology.
- Paribok frames the Zograf Readings as the long-dominant annual indological
  conference and describes other readings, including Roerich, as more
  chamber-like by comparison.
- The Sementsov Readings are presented as a Moscow/RUDN initiative focused on
  philosophical Sanskrit texts, interpretation, and contact with Indian
  scholarly environments.
- The source gives insider qualitative support for the article's broader
  claim that Russian conference indology is not a single undifferentiated
  field, but a set of partially distinct venues and styles.
- It is useful for the article's discussion of philosophical Sanskrit-text
  work, methodological reflection, and the emergence of newer conference
  formats after the older Zograf/Roerich pair.

## What The PDF Does Not Support

- It does not change the article's Zograf/Roerich descriptive statistics.
- It should not be merged into the PPV article's permutation model, overlap
  counts, G1-G3 distribution, or L1/L2 theme tables.
- It does not by itself prove hidden selection mechanisms, gatekeeping, or
  complete institutional structure.
- It should not be used to infer the whole Sementsov participant network for
  this article. Any such work would belong to a separate project with its own
  programmes and participant lists.

## Recommended Article Use

The safest article use is a short discussion note or footnote:

> A newer Sementsov Readings series, founded after the period in which Zograf
> and Roerich became the two stable long-running observable venues, is best
> treated here as contextual evidence rather than part of the measured corpus.
> Paribok's 2026 review frames it as a new Moscow/RUDN initiative beside the
> older Petersburg Zograf tradition and emphasizes philosophical Sanskrit-text
> interpretation and direct contact with Indian scholarly environments.

## Excluded Future Expansion

Do not collect Sementsov data for the current article. Do not recompute
overlap, L1/L2, G1-G3, or permutation models with Sementsov. Do not treat
the Paribok review as a prompt to make the present study a three-venue
comparison.

If Sementsov Readings are studied later, they should be handled as a separate
side note, appendix, or independent article. That future project would need
its own corpus design based on programmes rather than this proceedings review.

## Future Work Around The Main Two-Venue Model

The following future improvements preserve the article's Zograf/Roerich
scope.

### Affiliation And Biography Layer

Future work may link programme city labels to verified affiliation and
biographical evidence. Use programme strings as public representation. Use
external sources only when dated and source-backed. Do not interpret
"city only" as "no institution". Keep uncertain cases in review queues rather
than turning them into article claims.

### Publication Conversion

Future work may track whether Zograf/Roerich talks became proceedings papers,
journal articles, book chapters, monographs, edited volumes, or recurring
research themes across several years. This should be treated as a new data
layer. It should not be inferred from talk titles alone.

### Video And Digital Availability

Future work may analyze which talks have video, which years and venues are
overrepresented in video, whether video availability changes the apparent
public archive, and which talks are verifiable beyond the printed programme.
This layer should not be treated as representative unless missingness is
explicitly modeled.

### Main-Venue Robustness

Future work may verify edge cases in Zograf/Roerich programme extraction,
preserve the distinction between programme record, actually delivered talk,
and later publication, keep 2026 Zograf marked as preliminary until it is no
longer right-censored, and rerun summary tables only for Zograf and Roerich.

### Contextual Sources

Contextual sources such as the Paribok PDF may be cited to show how insiders
describe the field, how newer conferences present themselves, and how
Zograf/Roerich are perceived in relation to other venues. They must not become
row-level quantitative evidence for the current article.

## Practical Rule

Use the Paribok PDF as a qualitative witness to the conference ecology around
the Zograf/Roerich article. Do not use it as quantitative evidence inside the
current article's two-venue model.
