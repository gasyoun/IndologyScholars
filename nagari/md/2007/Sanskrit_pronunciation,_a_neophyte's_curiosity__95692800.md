# Sanskrit pronunciation, a neophyte's curiosity

[Читать оригинальный тред в Google Groups](https://groups.google.com/d/msgid/nagari/a93a30f5-6d3a-4f28-a111-7b81795db731@b40g2000prf.googlegroups.com)

## 1. Duri — 2007-12-21 11:50:21

In Sanskrit there is no f. Sansrkit v sometimes results from u before

vowel, thus showing its semivocalic nature. Therefore there isn't a

fricative v either. Instead we have such sounds as ph and bh, the

latter being nearly unpronounceable and otherwise unknown from any

other language (at least that I know of). Is it possible that, v being

pronounced w, ph and bh were pronounced like f and v respectively?

## 2. Alexandre V. Elizariev — 2007-12-21 14:49:21

Phonetically it is possible, phonologically not.
If you have heard the Nepalese pronouncing the tatsama words, you can 
understand what I mean. To write "Virginia Blend" on the cigarette pack in 
devanagari, they actually write bhirginia.  And the like. So you can expect 
a local variant of pronunciation, however the phonemes will remain intact.

Jap
