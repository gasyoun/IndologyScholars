## 32. Serge — 2007-03-24 13:25:43

Размышление по поводу 45.
Поскольку पौरुष в значении  "мужество" или "человечность" с контекстом
как-то не вяжется, то с помощью Аптэ находим ещё одно значение этого
слова - "усилие, работа", т.е. это некоторое действие предпринятое
человеком (पुरुष).
т.о. अकृत्वा पौरुषम् не предприняв усилия, не сделав работу, не
пошевелив пальцем.
या श्रीः счастье, которое ... - т.е. счастье, которое (достигнуто),
अकृत्वा पौरुषम्
Конструкция किम् + (Instr.) ifaik означает "для чего ... ?",
"зачем ... ?", "что толку в ... ?" и т.п.
सुभोग्य - легко получаемый, с удовольствием поедаемый
किम् तया अपि सुभोग्यया - что-то типа "зачем же оно легкодоступное?"
В другом варианте भाग्या - либо f. от भाग्य (to be shared or divided
divisible), либо по заверению МВ иногда = भाग्यम् (fate,  destiny,
fortune good fortune, luck, happiness, welfare).
Т.о. किम् तया अलस-भाग्यया - что-то типа "Зачем же оно, достающееся
ленивцам?", либо "Зачем же это счастье ленивых?"

Вторая часть стиха вполне понятна - про никчёмного старого буйвола или
антилопу, которым успешно удаётся сокращающать травяные ресурсы
планеты.

Т.о. концепция данной шлоки видится мне в следующем ключе:
Нам без пота и без крови на халяву мёд не нужен.
Только старый мерин щиплет богом посланную травку.
(Вариант прочтения: старый мерин курит.)

Лично мне такая философия кажется весьма извращённой и вредной. Грех
чураться халявы, будь ты хоть старая кляча, хоть геркулес. Хотя надо
признать, что как идеологический лозунг для стимуляции
производительности рабочего класса может и прокатить.

p.s. Буде тут случатся любители немецкой мовы, может они разъяснят мне
неучёному, что подразумевал Бётлинк в переводи этой шлоки под
отсутствующим в словаре словом Theil?

## 48. narayan prasad — 2007-04-03 07:56:28

O.K.



I explain herewith step-by-step. Please translate my explanation in Russian
so that those who do not know English may be in a position to understand it.
Before I go for explanation, I should express my limitation. I am basically
an Engineer by profession and have studied Panini on my own.



Now let us come to explanation of the two words सेव्यानि and सद्यःप्राणहराणि.
Your confusion lay in not understanding the form आनि in the verbal roots
सेव् & हृ (or हर्).



Most probably you were thinking that the word-forms सेव्यानि & हराणि were
finite verbs, i.e. तिङन्त forms of these roots. But actually they are
कृदन्तwith further
सुप् (nominal) suffixes.



Consider the word-forms सेव्य & प्राणहर. I think you do not have problem
with सद्यः

which is an अव्यय.

(A) सेव्य

(1) सेव्य = सेव् + the कृत् (also कृत्य) suffix ण्यत्

  Since the root सेव्  ends in a हल् (consonant), we can apply the sUtra
ऋहलोर्ण्यत् P.3.1.124.

      After removing the इत् markers ण् & त्, the suffix ण्यत् becomes य.
The sUtras dealing with इत् markers are: P.3.2.2-9.

(2) Since ण्यत्  is a कृत्य suffix (refer to P.3.1.95 whose अधिकार goes up
to 3.1.133), the word-form सेव्य can be used only in कर्मवाच्य (passive
voice). Please refer to the sUtra P.3.4.70 "तयोरेव कृत्य-क्त-खलर्थाः " and
the meaning given by me in my earlier message.

(3) ण्यत् is also a कृत् suffix (refer to P.3.1.93 whose अधिकार goes up to
3.4.76). So as per the sUtra P.1.2.46 "कृत्-तद्धित-समासाश्च" the word form
सेव्य is a प्रातिपदिक (nominal base). Therefore the sUtra 4.1.2 "सु-औ-जस्
-....-सुप् " becomes applicable.



(B) प्राणहर

(1) प्राणहर = प्राणं हरति इति कर्मणि उपपदे अच् प्रत्ययः (P.3.2.9 "
हरतेरनुद्यमनेऽच् ").

     The अनुवृत्ति of कर्मणि of P.3.2.1 runs up to P.3.2.58, which is used
in the sense of "one does that", i.e. the action indicated by the verbal
root. Here, "one takes away life" --- in this sense the कृत्  suffix
अच्comes after the root
हृ (to take away).

Since the अच् suffix is a कृत्  suffix (but not a कृत्य one), we can apply
the sUtra P.3.4.67 "कर्तरि कृत् ", i.e. here active voice and not the
passive is applicable.



(2) Since अच् suffix is neither णित् nor ञित्, वृद्धि of the root हृ is
ruled out.

(3) Since अच् suffix is neither तिङ् nor शित्. So it is not a
सार्वधातुकsuffix (cf
P.3.4.113). Apply the sUtra P.3.4.114 "आर्धधातुकं शेषः ", so the अच् suffix
is an आर्धधातुक suffix.

(4) Now apply the sUtra P.7.3.84 "सार्वधातुकार्धधातुकयोः ", leading to
guNation of the root हृ to become हर् . Now add the suffix अच् (= अ after
removing the इत् markers) and it becomes हर and the whole word as प्राणहर .

(5) अच् is a कृत् suffix (refer to P.3.1.93 whose अधिकार goes up to 3.4.76).
So as per the sUtra P.1.2.46 "कृत्-तद्धित-समासाश्च" the word form प्राणहर is
a प्रातिपदिक (nominal base). Therefore the sUtra 4.1.2 "सु-औ-जस्-....-सुप् "
becomes applicable.

A subhaashita is an unwarranted advice given in verses.
Many of them have deep meaning, but it is not a necessity.
Seriously Sandeep:
Literally, Subhashita means Good speech. Subhashitas cannot be
straitjacketed into a specific "category" of literature: they're
merely a collection of wise sayings, mostly in verse. The authorship
does not rest with a single person. However, the celebrated collection
of Subashitas is the one authored by Bhartuhari, the Shataka
(Shataka=100) trilogy: Neeti Shataka, Shrungara Shataka, and Vairagya
Shataka. Subhashitas are drawn from real life observations, a
sympathetic understanding of human impulses and behaviour, and some
generally reflect the state of affairs of the society at the time they
were composed. As such, they generally hold good-and true "till
eternity." The greatest value of Subhashitas is their ability to
provide comfort and hope in difficult times. No copious reading of
self-help books will, it is my conviction, convey the requisite wisdom
contained in these priceless verses. Self-help books do not contain
wisdom: they're a collection of "specific strategies (sic)" to
overcome this or that problem or to deal better with people or boost
your self-esteem.... You therefore have a user manual for every
conceivable situation/subject/topic under the sun. Ask a person who
has read these user manuals to what extent his/her life has
"dramatically improved" as a result. Self-help books are like drugs:
the high lasts as long as you're hooked to the pages.

शी अते ---> शे अते (७.४.२१) ---> शे र् अते (७.१.६) ---> शेरते
