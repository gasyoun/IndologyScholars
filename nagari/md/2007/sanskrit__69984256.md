## 2. Marcis — 2007-12-23 19:14:51

It is the same varnamala. Just a few (prakritic and simplified)
changes:
1) several additional modified letters to represent Arabic sounds
2) instead of ligatures simple consonant clusters are used
3) only the northern (Deli) - variants of vowels
4) "a" is not spelled in the end of most words (without virama)

## 6. Serge — 2007-12-24 13:43:12

Could you give an example showing clear difference between the
Sanskrit and Hindi ligature style? I do not imagine how you can
simplify the ligature "tra" for example, or "hna", or "ShTra", or
"j~na", or "ddha" etc. In any case the more or less complicated style
depends only on technical capabilities or on an individual
handwriting. You can't say that in Hindi you must use simplified
ligatures, and in Sanskrit - more complicated. Sanskrit can be written
in simplified style as well. And in Hindi if you wish you can use the
most complicated ligatures. Nobody can prohibit it.

The only difference I know in Hindi vs. Sanskrit ligature-writting is
that in Hindi a nasal followed by a consonant is not written as the
nasal consonant but is always replaced by the anuswara sign, while in
Sanskrit it can be written both ways.
