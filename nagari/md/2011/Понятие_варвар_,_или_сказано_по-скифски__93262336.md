Словарь Дворецкого
I βάρβᾰρος 2
1)	варварский, т. е. негреческий, иноземный (φωνή Aesch.; γλῶσσα
Soph.; πόλις Thuc.; τάξις Plut.): βάρβαρος πόλεμος Thuc. война с
иноземцами;
2)	непонятный, невнятный (βάγματα Aesch.);
3)	изъясняющийся на неправильном греческом языке Luc.;
4)	варварский, некультурный, грубый (ἀμαθὴς καὶ βάρβαρος Arph.; sc.
ἔθνη Xen.).
II βάρβᾰρος ὁ варвар, т. е. не грек, чужеземец Aesch., Eur., Her.,
Thuc., Xen., Plat., Arst., Plut., Luc.
βαρβᾰρόομαι
1)	уподобляться варварам: βεβαρβάρωσαι χρόνιος ὢν ἐν βαρβάροις Eur.
прожив долго среди варваров, ты сам стал варваром;
2)	становиться непонятным (οἶστρος κακὸς καὶ βεβαρβαρωμένος Soph.).
βαρβᾰρικῶς
1)	по-варварски, т. е. не по-гречески (κεκλημένος Arst.), преимущ. по-
персидски (ἐβόα Xen.);
βαρβᾰρίζω
2)	говорить на плохом или ломаном греческом языке Plat., Arst., Plut.;
3)	быть на стороне варваров, т. е. сочувствовать персам Xen.

Griechisches Etymologisches Woerterbuch [Frisk] :
Latin transcription: bárbaros
Greek: βάρβαρος, -ον
Grammatical information: Subst. m. und Adj.
Meaning: `Ausländer, ausländisch, Nicht-Grieche, ungriechisch', auch
`ungebildet, roh' (ion. att., bei Homer im Komp. βαρβαρόφωνος, von den
Karern Β 867).
Derivatives: Ableitungen: βαρβαρικός `ausländisch, fremd' (Simon.,
Th., X., Arist. usw.) mit βαρβαρίκιον N. eines Kleidungsstückes
(Pap.); βαρβαρώδης (Sch., Tz.). - Denominative Verba: 1. βαρβαρίζω
`sich auf Barbarenweise betragen', bes. in bezug auf die Rede, `es mit
den B., d. h. den Persern halten' (Hdt., X., Arist., hell.) mit
βαρβαρισμός `der Gebrauch fremder Sprache und Sitte,
Sprachfehler' (Arist., hell.) und dem Adv. βαρβαριστί `in barbarischer
Weise, Sprache' (Ar., Plu. u. a.). 2. βαρβαρόομαι `zum Barbaren
werden, verwildern' (S., E. u. a.).
Etymological information: Onomatopoetische Reduplikationsbildung, mit
aind. (nachved.) barbara- `stammelnd', pl. Bez. nichtarischer Völker,
identisch. Ebenso sumer. barbar `Ausländer', sem.-babyl. barbaru `der
Fremde'. Nach Weidner Glotta 4, 303f., Specht KZ 66, 11 und Lexis 3,
70 stammen βάρβαρος und aind. barbara- aus babyl.-sumer. Quelle. Aus
βάρβαρος lat. barbarus. Das Wort hat sich gewiß zuerst auf die Sprache
bezogen, s. Specht a. a. O. (gegen Weidner) und Schwyzer 78 mit A. 5.
- Über ähnliche Bildungen in anderen idg. Sprachen WP. 2, 105f., Pok.
91f.  Pages: 1,219-220

## 2. Arvind_Kolhatkar — 2011-10-24 19:58:50

Friends,

This is my first active venture into this learned Group, though I have
been silently observing its discussions for quite some time.  I
understand a little Russian, having studied it 50 years ago.  It has
become somewhat rusty because of disuse but is good enough so that I
can follow the drift of what is written in it.

For what it is worth, here is what Monier-Williams says about the word
बर्बर. According to him, it is also written as वर्वर.  Among the
several meanings given by him is 'non-Aryans, barbarians' and he cites
महाभारत as the place where it is used in this sense.

In modern Hindi, the बर्बरता is used to denote cruelty, unconscionable
behavior and the like.

The North African coast was known to the Europeans as the Barbary
Coast because of the Berber Tribes there captured ships belonging to
European nations and sold the Christian sailors into slavery.  Ancient
Indians did not have a similar dread of the Berber Tribes because,
being far away from them, they had no experience or perhaps even
knowledge of the ways of the Berber Tribes.  Yet, Sanskrit has the
same word with the same meaning.  I, for one, am unable to account for
Sanskrit came to possess the same meaning of the word.

Arvind Kolhatkar, Toronto, October 24, 2011.

## 3. Stormwatsch — 2011-10-26 11:34:05

> I, for one, am unable to account for
> Sanskrit came to possess the same meaning of the word.

It's all very simple, the word concerned is of Proto-Indo-European
origin. The PIE base *barbar- is echoic of unintelligible speech of
foreigners, hence the Greek meaning "non-Greek-speaking foreigners"
and Sanskrit meanings "stammering", "non-Arian".

As for the Berber people, their name for themselves in their Berber
language is "Imazighen" (singular: "Amazigh"), the word "Berber" being
the name which Romans used to refer to them.

According to Greeks, Romans were also considered "Barbarians", but
when they adopted Greek culture, Romans began to use the word
"barbarus" to name those who weren't of Greek or Roman origin,
including the African Imazighen people.
So, the name of the African people is of Greek origin, and both Greek
and Sanskrit words come from the Proto-Indo-European language.

## 4. Brahman — 2011-10-26 11:46:59

ВАРВАР
(лат. barbarus, от греч. barboros - чужестранец). 1) у древних греков
и римлян - всякий иностранец, иноплеменник. 2) теперь - грубый,
жестокий человек; не культурный.
Словарь иностранных слов, вошедших в состав русского языка.- Чудинов
А.Н., 1910.

ВАРВАР
1) всякий иностранец у древн. греков, вроде нашего простонародного
«немец»; 2) челов. грубый, невежественный, жестокий.
Полный словарь иностранных слов, вошедших в употребление в русском
языке.- Попов М., 1907.

ВАРВАР
[гр. barbaros] - у древних греков и римлян: всякий чужеземец. Перен.
невежественный, грубый, жестокий человек.
Словарь иностранных слов.- Комлев Н.Г., 2006.
