Это грамматика, практически анаголичная Панини. Некоторые утверждают, что 

некоторые разделы грамматики даже лучше, чем у Панини.

ведийского санскрита. Но это можно найти в отдельных книгах или у того-же 

Панини.

Вот что Наш учитель говорит об авторе и книге.



jīva gosvāmī studied an astounding number of grammatical treatises. indeed 

based on the various references he gives in his vṛttis [автокомментарий], 

we know that he studied pāṇini’s aṣṭādhyāyī along with its various 

commentaries such as kātyāyana’s vārttikas, patañjali’s mahā-bhāṣya, vāmana 

and jayāditya’s kāśikā, and bhāṣā-vṛtti and bhāga-vṛtti. we also know that 

he studied Zarvavarma ācārya’s kātantra-vyākaraṇa, vopadeva’s 

mugdha-bodha-vyākaraṇa, kramadīśvara’s saṅkṣipta-sāra-vyākaraṇa, candra 

gomī’s cāndra-vyākaraṇa, padmanābha datta’s supadma-vyākaraṇa, anubhūti 

svarūpācārya’s sārasvata-vyākaraṇa, and rāmacandra ācārya’s 

prakriyā-kaumudī along with their respective commentaries which are too 

numerous to name. out of these, kātantra-vyākaraṇa, sārasvata-vyākaraṇa, 

and so on are incomplete by themselves, sārasvata-vyākaraṇa and 

prakriyā-kaumudī, which is the first rearrangement of pāṇini’s sūtras into 

topical divisions, are fraught with mistakes, and pāṇini’s aṣṭādhyāyī 

itself is so concise and the language so difficult that nobody can 

understand it without multiple commentaries. for example, learning the 

rules of grammar in the pāṇinian system reṝuires first reading pāṇini’s 

sūtras, then considering the additions and amendments found in the 

vārttikas, and then reading the great commentary called mahā-bhāṣya. but 

then, if one wants to understand every sūtra deeply, one has to also study 

kāśikā and it’s subcommentaries nyāsa and pada-mañjarī, since mahā-bhāṣya 

only comments on one third of pāṇini’s sūtras. at present, people generally 

study pāṇini’s grammar through the medium of siddhānta-kaumudī, a book 

written by bhaṭṭojī dīkṣita (a contemporary of jīva gosvāmī) which 

rearranges pāṇini’s sūtras into topical divisions. however the ambiguity 

and confusion still remains because the natural anuvṛtti of pāṇini’s sūtras 

is broken when their order is changed. jīva gosvāmī’s grammar, however, is 

unparalleled because he painstakingly collects the essential teachings of 

all these grammatical works and puts them together in one place in a clear, 

precise, and kṛṣṇa-conscious way. there is no ambiguity and the sūtras are 

designed in such a way that they speak their only meaning without the need 

of multiple commentaries. thus jīva gosvāmī’s grammar shines above all 

other grammars both in terms of grammatical content and spiritual value.
