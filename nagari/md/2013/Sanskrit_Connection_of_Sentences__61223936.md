Connection of Sentences Coordination  



Sometimes sentences are put together as they are, often expressing an 

antithesis.

 

(1)

  

 



Example:



Na te doṣaḥ ayam svāminaḥ doṣaḥ. (1)



‘This is not your fault, but that of your master.’



 



Otherwise, coordination is done with different particles or pronouns, e.g.:

  

Demonstrative:

 

ca ‘and’, api ‘and’, atha ‘now’

 

(2)

  

Disjunctive:

 

vā ‘or’

 

(3)

  

Antithesis:

 

tu ‘but’, param ‘however’, punaḥ ‘again’, atha ‘also’

 

(4)

  

Causal:

 

hi ‘because’

 

(5)

  

Conclusive:

 

tat or tasmāt ‘therefore’, tataḥ ‘thence’, ataḥ ‘hence’, tarhi ‘in that 

case’, 

 

(6)

  

 

  

These particles are also used within sentences. 

 

(7)

  

 



Examples:



Priyaṃvadaka, viśramyatām parijananena. Tvam api svam adhikāram aśūnyam 

kuru. (2)



‘Priyaṃvadaka, my attendants may ceep their rest and you, discharge your 

duty.’



Strīyaḥ hi nāma khalu enāḥ nisargāt eva paṇḍitaḥ. Puruṣāṇām tu pāṇḍityam 

śāstraiḥ eva upadiśyate (4)



‘Womankind, indeed, are wise by nature, but to men wisdom is to be taught 

by manuals.’

Subordination  



Subordinated sentences are caracterized by the relative ya or one of its 

many derivatives, e.g. yena<http://www.warnemyr.com/skrgram/grammar/morphology/pronouns.html#rel>, 

yatra <http://www.warnemyr.com/skrgram/grammar/morphology/pronouns.html#rel>, 

yāvat <http://www.warnemyr.com/skrgram/grammar/morphology/pronouns.html#rel>, 

yādṛśa<http://www.warnemyr.com/skrgram/grammar/morphology/pronouns.html#rel>, 

yataḥ <http://www.warnemyr.com/skrgram/grammar/morphology/pronouns.html#rel>and 

yathā <http://www.warnemyr.com/skrgram/grammar/morphology/pronouns.html#rel>. 

(Collectivly known here as ‘relatives’.)

 

(8)

  

 



The following rules apply:

  

The demonstrative is chiefly saḥ, tat, sā



 (9)

  

The demonstrative and relative need not be of the same type.

 

(10)

  

The relative sentence is often put first.

 

(11)

  

The relative may be part of a compound

 

(12)

  

The relative may depend on a Abs, Part or an absolute case.

 

(13)

  

The relative is as rule put first in the sentence

 

(14)

  

In general propositions the relative sentence is often characterized by two 

or more relatives put close together.

 

(15)

  

 



Examples:



Yeṣām tu yādṛśam karma bhūtānām iha kīrtitam, 



tat tayā vaḥ abhidhāsyāmi. (9, 10, 11 & 14)



‘Now, what duties are assigned to the different beings in this world,



I will tell you.’



Sā aham etat na veda, yat-gotraḥ tvam asi. (9, 10, 11 & 12)



‘I do not know what gotra you are.’



Yaḥ yat jayati, tasya tat. (15)



‘That which one captures, is one’s own.’

Direct Construction with iti 



(aka oratorio recta)



 



Construction:

  

The outward forms of word and ideas are reproduced unaltered and followed 

by iti.

 

(16)

  

It usually preceeds the main sentence but when it follows it is often 

introduced by yat<http://www.warnemyr.com/skrgram/grammar/morphology/pronouns.html#rel>or 

yathā <http://www.warnemyr.com/skrgram/grammar/morphology/pronouns.html#rel>without changing its direct character.

 

(17)

  

Sometimes demonstratives as evam, ittham or Pron as eṣa, ayam, idam 

replaces iti.

 

(18)

  

Sometimes iti is just left out.

 

(19)

  

 



Uses:

  

Expressing the object of knowing, thinking, believing, reflecting, 

doubting, rejoicing, wondering, etc. (These verbs are often left out but 

indicated by iti.)

 

(20)

  

Denotes the fact wich acts as a cause or motive

 

(21)

  

Signifying the object of purpose or wish

 

(22)

  

 



Examples:



Jeṣyāmaḥ iti ucyante. (16)



‘We will conquer, so they said.’



Tam vada yad-anyaḥ bhṛtyaḥ vāhanāyāḥ mat-sthāne kriyatām. (17)



‘Tell him, he must appoint some other of his servants, instead of me, 



to be his carrier.’



Svāmī evam vadati: cirāt dṛśyate. (18)



‘My master speaks thus: “it is long ago since I saw you”.’



Vane jalam iti Devadattena dhrutaḥ. (20)



‘[Beleiving that] there was water in the forest, Devadatta run away.’
