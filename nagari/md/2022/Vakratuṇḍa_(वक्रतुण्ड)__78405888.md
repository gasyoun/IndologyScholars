Vakratuṇḍa (वक्रतुण्ड) by Nityānanda Miśra
You most likely know ‘vakratuṇḍa’ (वक्रतुण्ड) refers to Gaṇeśa. But did you 
know that a parrot is also called ‘vakratuṇḍa’ (वक्रतुण्ड) in Sanskrit? 
Here are nine Sanskrit words for a parrot—
‘śuka’ (शुक): “one who shines or radiates”, source of Hindi ‘suggā’ 
(सुग्गा) and ‘sūā’ (सूआ)
‘kīra (कीर): “one who utters [the sound] ‘ki’”, In the ‘Naiṣadhīyacarita’, 
Śrīharṣa describes the haṃsa speaking to Damayantī as “स 
कीरवन्मानुषवागवादीत्” (“with human speech, he spoke like a parrot”)
‘vakratuṇḍa’ (वक्रतुण्ड): “one with a curved beak”. The word ‘tuṇḍa’ 
(तुण्ड) in Sanskrit can refer to the mouth of a human, the snout of a 
mammal, the beak of a bird, and the trunk of an elephant.
‘medhāvī’ (मेधावी): “intelligent”, as parrots can [feign to] converse like 
humans
‘dāḍimapriya’ (दाडिमप्रिय): “one who is fond of [eating] pomegranate 
[seeds]”
‘raktatuṇḍa’ (रक्ततुण्ड): “one whose beak is red [in colour]”
‘vakracañcu’ (वक्रचञ्चु): “one with a curved beak”
‘cimi’ (चिमि): “one who chooses [what to eat or speak]”
‘cimika’ (चिमिक): same meaning as above
