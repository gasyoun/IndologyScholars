The Cambridge Greek Lexicon also begins each entry with the rootmeaning of 

a word, a fundamentally different approach to the 19th-century lexicon, 

which started entries with a word’s earliest appearance in literature. 

“Take a word like πόλις, which will be familiar to many in its English form 

‘polis’,” said Diggle. “Our article shows the variety of senses which the 

word can have: in its earliest usage ‘citadel, acropolis’; then, more 

generally, ‘city, town’ and also ‘territory, land’; and, more specifically, 

in the classical period, ‘city as a political entity, city-state’; also, 

with reference to the occupants of a city, ‘community, citizen body’.”
