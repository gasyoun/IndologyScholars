## 2. Mari Kadanik — 2021-03-03 20:08:21

Hi,



there are two large (text) categories in the Vedic tradition - śruti

("heard") and smṛti ("remembered").

The former is the revelation that opened to the ṛṣi's (great seers, sages)

in their deep meditations as revelation, these are the Vedic mantras, which

are till now passed on from guru to disciple exactly the way they were

heard. These texts do not have human authorship.

The latter encompasses all the other Indian scriptures with known human

authors, which classically were based on the Vedas and expounded their

content further. So, we are talking about a vast body of texts, to outline

this material, one probably has to study the whole Indian classic

literature.





Mari

## 4. Mari Kadanik — 2021-03-04 17:22:23

As far as I know, very classically only Vedas itself is considered to be

śruti.

The only text traditionally equalled to them is Bhagavad Gita of Vyasa,

because it sums up the essence of the Vedic teaching and at the same time

is itself a revelation of Lord Krishna. Although very technically it still

belongs to smṛti category.

In another view, very essentially, (some) Shaiva Agamas can be considered

as śruti. The great authoritative Tamil saint and siddha Tirumuular, the

author of the great spiritual work "Tirumandiram", asserts Shaiva Agamas to

be the direct revelation of Shiva.





Mari
