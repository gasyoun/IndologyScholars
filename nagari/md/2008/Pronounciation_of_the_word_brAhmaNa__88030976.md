Hello All,



The word brAhmaNa is pronounced as brAmhaNa (at least in south India).

Is there any pramANa for this interchange of 'mha' to 'hma' in

pronunciation either in grammatical works or "shiksha" works ?



Many regards

Venkatesh

## 2. Vasu Srinivasan — 2009-01-04 21:26:47

Venkatesh,

Yes there is.

The sutra "hakArannaNamaparanAsikAyaM"  is from taittirya prAtisakhya . The
prAtisakhya provides sutras for pronounciations.

By this rule, vahni - vanhi, gRhNoti - gRNhoti, brahma - bramha are all
valid. While writing it is still written brahma, but pronounciation bramha
is valid.

This prAtisakhya is mainly chanted by Krishna Yajur Vedins.

## 3. N.S. VenkataNarasimha — 2009-01-10 13:33:53

Dear Mr. Srinivasan

Thanks for the reply.

I belong to Tattiriya shakha and am quite familiar with the prAtishAkhya.

The said sutra (21.14), in the view of the commentaries tribhAShyaratna,
vaidikAbharaNa, and padakramasAdana (of mAhiSheya),
introduces an anunAsika 'Ha'kAra after the Ha-kAra when the later is
followed by na/ma/Na. It in no way explains the swapping of consonants in
pronunciation.

The sUtra can in fact be interpreted to mean " Ha-kAra, occurring before
na/ma/Na, transports itself as anunAsika Ha-kAra, towards the end of
anunAsika (ie. na/ma/Na)".

But this is against all the three commentaries which explain the sUtra in
the context of yamAkSharAs, which according to some AchAryAs includes
anunAsika-HakAra.

Also, the tribhAShyaratna says this sutra is undesirable to the
prAtishAkhya.

I believe there was a conglomeration of pandits in north Bharata during
British raj, to decide on the pronunciation, which finally decided that the
swapping in pronunciation is valid.

Pl. note that many pandits in north Bharata do  prnounce the consonants as
they occur in the written from.

Sri Bannanje Govindacharya, a veteran scholar in sanskrit (in Bangalore,
Karnataka), rejected the idea of alteration in pronounciation as un-founded
and chooses to prnounce the consonants as the occur in written form (ie, as
brahma etc.).

None of the shiksha's in shiksha-sangraha, not
'VaidikashikShAsvarUpavimarshaH' have any reference to this effect.

While there is no doubt regarding the authenticity of swapped
pronounciation, the pamANa does not seem to be very apparent.

May be one should look at varNakrama for a conclusive proof.

Thanks and regards
Venkatesh
