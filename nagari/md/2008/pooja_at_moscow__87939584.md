## 8. Vasu Srinivasan — 2008-04-25 21:54:04

Interesting question. I will reply in English, as Im not used to Russian
keys in a long time.

There is no "document proof" that a person is a "Brahmin". It is the
knowledge of Vedas that is proof by itself. A brahmin typically undergoes
"upanayana" in his early age and is supposed to learn, though the
"sacred-thread" has been common and is common in "Kshatriya" and "Vaishya"
castes too.

A lineage is oneway of identifying who the brahmin's guru was/is. The people
who conduct rituals, like pujas, wedding, upanayana, homa-s etc are priests
- who are typically "affiliated" to a Matha (maTa) or to Temples, though its
not a necessity. There are several maTa-s, like Sringeri, Kanchi, Udupi,
Ahobila, Puri, Mantralaya etc. But in most cases priests are associated to
one or more temples, which is what their job is (or supposed to be).

Association to temples, knowledge of vedas, doing daily pujas at the temples
and doing nityakarma (daily brahminical duties) is more or less a
qualification.

- Vasya

## 13. Vasu Srinivasan — 2008-04-28 21:35:42

Let me take the liberty to translate acharya-ji's reply to English.

---
In Kali yuga, a  brahmin is born by birth. There is no necessity for him to
have a document or have any other qualifications. But just because born as a
brahmin, if he does not do brahma-karma (reciting vedas, pujas, other nitya
karmas, incl. living only by dakshina etc) then he is a "brahmin" only by
name.
----

Just to clarify, the word "brahman" has many mis-uses in the context of
spelling. Here are few corrections (notice the stresses):

brahmaN The supreme brahman, which is the source of creation (in terms of
vedanta) (from the root bruh - to expand/contract).
brAhmaNa: one who "knows" or one whose purpose is to attain brahmaN
brahmin: English equivalent of "brAhmaNa:" (just like sanskrita became
sanskrit)
brahman: (as used by acharya-ji above, same as brAhmaNa:, but in Hindi, the
final "a:" gets dropped)
