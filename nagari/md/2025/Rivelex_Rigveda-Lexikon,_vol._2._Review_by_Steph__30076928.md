This is the second volume of the extremely ambitious Rivelex project, 

intended to produce a new dictionary of the Rig Veda to replace the 

indispensable Wörterbuch of Hermann Grassmann first published in 1875. The 

first volume was published in 2006, was even longer than this one (746 

pages of lemmata), and covered only words beginning with short a. As has 

also been noted in other reviews of this work, judging from the size of the 

volumes and the rate at which they are being produced, the complete 

dictionary will be available not to us, not to our current students, but, 

with luck, to their students (though, to be safe, I would probably insert 

another generation). As I did not review the first volume, this review will 

comment on both volumes and on the project as a whole.

There is no doubt that Grassmann needs to be replaced: Rigvedic philology 

has made huge advances in the almost 150 years since its publication-most 

importantly in determining and refining the meaning of lexical items and in 

analyzing the trickier parts of the morphology (which often involves 

assembling into a single paradigm forms that appear under separate lemmata 

in Grassmann [e.g., his rayé and rê, râ, both 'Gut, Schatz, Reichtum']). I 

am sure that everyone who works at all regularly on the Rig Veda has his or 

her own ideas about how best to update it-though I'm pretty sure that 

everyone would agree that the infamous "Grassmann numbers," which do not 

match the numbering of the Samhita text and make it cumbersome to locate 

passages referred to in the Wörterbuch, would have to go. (An improvement 

Rivelex has indeed made.) But I very much doubt that anyone's "dream" 

neo-Grassmann would come out as Rivelex.

An extraordinary amount of learning, effort, and care has gone into the 

making of these two volumes, and one can only salute Thomas Krisch and his 

Mitarbeiter (innen) for their dedication. But I am not certain a comparable 

amount of thought went into considering what is especially useful about 

Grassmann and should be kept and what new features would complement what 

was held over. The result, at least for me, is a work that I cannot figure 

out how, or why, to use-unlike other fairly recent publications on the Rig 

Veda, notably A. Lubotsky's 1997 Rigvedic Word Concordance.
