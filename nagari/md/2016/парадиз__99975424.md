## 2. Marina Savelieva — 2016-01-17 23:01:12

The word "paradise" entered English from the French
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language> *paradis*, inherited from
the Latin <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language> *paradisus*, from
Greek <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language> *parádeisos* (παράδεισος),
from an Old Iranian <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Iranian>
**paridayda-* "walled enclosure". By the 6th/5th century BCE, the Old
Iranian word had been adopted as Assyrian
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language>*pardesu* "domain". It
subsequently came to indicate walled estates, especially the carefully
tended royal parks and menageries <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menagerie>.
The term eventually appeared in Greek as *parádeisos* "park for animals" in
the *Anabasis <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabasis_(Xenophon)>* of the
early 4th century BCE Athenian Xenophon
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophon>. Aramaic
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic_language>*pardaysa* similarly
reflects "royal park".

Hebrew <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language> פרדס (*pardes*)
appears thrice in the Tanakh <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanakh>;
in the Song
of Solomon <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_Solomon> 4:13,
Ecclesiastes <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastes> 2:5 and Nehemiah
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Nehemiah> 2:8. In those contexts it
could be interpreted as an "orchard" or a "fruit garden". In the Septuagint
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septuagint> (3rd–1st centuries BCE), Greek
παράδεισος *parádeisos* was used to translate both Hebrew *pardes* and
Hebrew *gan*, "garden": it is from this usage that the use of "paradise" to
refer to the Garden of Eden
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_of_Eden> derives.
The same usage also appears in Arabic
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language> and in the Quran
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran> as *firdaws
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firdaws>* فردوس.

The word's etymology is ultimately derived from a PIE root
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PIE_root> **dheigʷ* "to stick and set
up".[*citation
needed <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed>*] It is
reflected in Avestan <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avestan_language> as
*pairi-daêza-*.[1]
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise#cite_note-New_Oxford-1>[2]
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise#cite_note-2> The literal meaning of
this Eastern Old Iranian language word is "walled (enclosure)",[1]
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise#cite_note-New_Oxford-1> from
*pairi-* "around" and *-diz* "to make, form (a wall), build".[3]
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise#cite_note-3>[4]
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise#cite_note-4> The word is not
attested in other Old Iranian languages (these may however be
hypothetically reconstructed, for example as Old Persian
<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian> **paridayda-*). The idea of a
walled enclosure was not preserved in most Iranian usage, and generally
came to refer to a plantation or other cultivated area, not necessarily
walled. For example, the Old Iranian word survives in New Persian *pālīz* (or
"jālīz"), which denotes a vegetable patch.

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise#Etymology
