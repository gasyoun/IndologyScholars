Dear Raghavendra jii and other members,



     For learners of Panini Skt Grammar,  who do not have any teacher to
guide, I would suggest the following:



(I)                   Never consult any Kaumudii works (whether LSK, MSK,
or VSK) in the beginning, because you will never be in a position to
understand the meaning of any Panini sUtra with these works. This I
emphatically declare from my own experience.

(II)                 First try to understand the *meaning of all the sUtras
from the sUtras themselves* with all the related anuvRttis taken into
account. To save your precious time, first try to understand only the
function of each sUtra in the derivation of examples cited in the appendix
of the Hindi/English *commentary of the Astadhyayi*. You can go for
complete derivation later.

(III)                If you can read Hindi, please consult/purchase the
Hindi commentary of Astadhyayi by Pt Brahmadatta Jijnaasu. The full postal
address for getting this book in 3 Vols is already supplied by me in
earlier message.

(IV)              If you cannot read Hindi, you can consult the English
commentary of Astadhyayi in 6 Vols by Rama Nath Sharma. This book has been
published by Munshiram Manoharlal, Delhi. This book is rather costly (about
Rs seven thousand), but it gives complete derivational history like the
Hindi commentary mentioned in (III) above. Unfortunately, it uses Romanized
Sanskrit (except the sUtrapATha), which may be very boring for many people.

(V)                In addition, please purchase also the book
"mAdhaviiyaa-dhAtuvRtti" (in Sanskrit) edited by Dr Vijayapal. This book
gives important Panini sUtras in the derivation of all the dhAtus. The
publisher is Ramlal Kapoor Trust.

(VI)              If you feel like, you can also keep a manual called
"rUpa-chandrikA" (all the verb-forms of about two hundred important dhAtus
in all the ten lakAras, except leT; also all the word-forms in all the
cases for about two hundred words). It has been published by Bharatiya
Vidya Prakashan, Delhi/Varanasi. There is another one of the same title but
different author, the publisher being Chaukhambha Sanskrit Sansthan.

(VII)             If you can read Hindi, you may also buy the
"VyaakaraN-Chandroday" (esp. Vol. III, dealing with verb-forms, out of
total 5 Vols) by Dr Charudev Shastry, Publisher is MLBD, Delhi. I am not
aware of any similar work in English.

(VIII)           The books "mAdhaviiyaa-dhAtuvRtti" &
"vyaakaraN-Chandroday" should be consulted preferably after having studied
Astadhyayi up to first 3 chapters.



*<< I am serious Sanskrit learning student. But due to working hour's
schedule, I am finding difficulty in spending vast amount of time in
understanding the difficult thing of grammar.>>*



BTW, I have my basic qualification in Engg and working in the same field. I
formally studied basic Sanskrit up to High School. But I had never seen
Astadhyayi. It was only after seven years in service that I started
studying Panini in August 1987. I would study all this at night (beyond my
office hours) and during holidays. In the beginning I started with
Mahabharata (and some other small books) for examples. I would myself try
to derive each new verb-form that I came across. In about one year I could
get sufficient working knowledge of Panini. In January 1991 I came across
the sarasvatiikaNThaabharaNa of   Bhojadeva. It is the complete revised
edition of Panini and has about 6432 sUtras as per Madras edition (1937).
......  ......



There was nobody to guide. So in the beginning I had also purchased LSK &
VSK (after seeing some advertisement in a small book on Sanskrit grammar
owned by another person working in my own office). I hardly understood the
meaning of any sUtra from these works. Soon I realized that these works
assume the knowledge of Astadhyayi, which unfortunately I didn't have. I
wrote to MLBD, Delhi to supply me Astadhyayi with Hindi commentary. By
chance, MLBD sent me the Hindi commentary of Astadhyayi by Pt Brahmadatta
Jijnaasu. So far I have not found any commentary (meant for beginners) in
any language better than this.



I hardly consulted LSK & VSK after having studied Astadhyayi. But I did
find "VyaakaraN-Chandroday" (Vol.III) very useful.





--- Narayan Prasad
