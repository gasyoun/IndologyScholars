*PRESENTATION OF A NEW SANSKRIT DICTIONARY*

 

 

 

The paper is a presentation of a new Sanskrit  dictionary: the 
Sanskrit-Catalan Dictionary published in December 2005 (Barcelona). The 
Dictionary contains more than 50.000 words, including compounds. It covers 
the period from the Upaniṣads to pauraṇika literature, with especial 
reference to philosophical and technical literature. Its has a size of 1161 
pages, excluding indexes, and contains more than 6 million characters. It 
is the result of 12 years of work both in India and in Spain.

 

         Prof. Michael Coulson in his book Sanskrit was of the opinion that 
existing Sanskrit dictionaries were ‘grossly out of date’, as the most 
important lexicographic activity regarding Sanskrit was done more than a 
hundred years ago. Since then both Indological Studies and Lexicography 
have greatly progressed. Consequently updating Sanskrit lexicography is a 
badly needed task.  The present sanscrit-català Dictionary is a small 
attempt in that direction.

 

         Among the new contributions of this dictionary we can list the 
following:

 

–Updating and reordering of Sanskrit lexical items according to the 
findings of the two etymological dictionaries of Manfred Mayrhofer: the 
Kurzgefasstess Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindischen (1956-1980) and 
the Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindischen (1986-2001). As a result a 
certain amount of words that in existing Sanskrit dictionaries appear in a 
single entry are considered as homonims with different etymologies and 
appear under different entries.

 

–Inclusion of Sanskrit technical terms from the main systems of philosophy, 
especially Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Vedānta. The terms have been 
extracted from prakaraṇas and other texts such as *Tarkasa**ṃ**graha*, 
*Tarkabhā**ṣ**a*, *Vedāntasāra*, *Vivekacū**ḍ**āma**ṇ**i*, *Sā**ṃ*
*khyakārikā* and *Yogasūtra* with several comentaries. Terms from the 
Sanskrit grammarians and Tantra have been also included but not in a 
systematic way.

 

–Two types of etymologies are consistently given in square brackets: those 
belonging to the field of comparative philology and the derivations 
proposed by the Indian grammarians. As in the Poona Dictionary the 
philological etymology is separated from the traditional one by the use of 
a black oblique line /. Reconstructed indo-European forms (usually in the 
form that appear in EWA or Pok) are given where necessary. Cognates forms 
are sparingly given to help the reader to identify family words. The 
traditional derivations given in the dictionary consist in an abbreviated 
representation that breaks the word into base and affixes according to the 
system of Pāṇini. Augments (*āgama*) and substitutes (*ādeśa*) are not 
included. The derivations are taken from traditional grammatical texts (
*Kāśikā*, *K**ṣ**īratara**ṅ**ginī*, *Rāmāśramin*, *Ga**ṇ**apā**ṭ**ha*, *U*
*ṇ**ādisūtra* etc.) and usually we have refrained from making our own 
derivations. 

 

–The Dictionary follows basic norms of modern lexicography, especially the 
distinction between translation and explanation. Existing Sanskrit 
dictionaries indiscriminately mix translations and explanations in their 
presentations. Moreover Sanskrit dictionaries are quite loose in their 
ordering of meanings giving long strings of words that are not synonyms 
without any typographical device to indicate shades of meanings. Thus, 
strings such as “frost, cold, snow, mist, dew, thin rain” have been 
properly arranged.

 

–In the arrangement of meanings the dictionary follows a historical 
principle, with period tags suchs as RV+, Up+, èp+, etc. An asterisc is 
also used to indicate the most frequent meanings of a word.
