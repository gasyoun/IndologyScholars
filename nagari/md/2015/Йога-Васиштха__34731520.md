## 2. Arvind_Kolhatkar — 2015-04-21 17:17:29

<Не подскажете ли, что это за зверь करभी  комментатор пояснил его как 
 भल्लूकी вроде похоже на медведя, или муравьед, но муравьед не ест змей. 
Это в Й-В в 17-й шлоке 21-й сарги 1-й пракараны, 
В комментарии так: 
करभ्यत्र भल्लूकी।
सा हि बिलस्थान्सर्पादिन्श्वासबलेनाकृष्य भक्षयतीति प्रसिद्धम्।>

'करभ' means 'trunk of an elephant' according to MW.  (Its other meaning is 
'हस्त’, which is the name of one of the 27 lunar mansions or नक्षत्रs of 
Hindu Astrology.  This mansion, coming towards the end of the four-month 
rainy season in India, is associated with heavy rain, whose falling lines 
of the rain जलधारा are as 'thick as the elephant trunk', hence the name, 
because हस्त also means the trunk of the elephant.) 

In करभ्यत्र भल्लूकी, 'भल्लूक' means the 'bear' медведь.  It may also mean 
'муравьед' or ant-eater, though as far as I know India does not have 
long-tongued ant-eaters of the South American type.  Indian bears, however, 
are reputed to be clever enough to insert a long twig into an ant-hill and 
eat ants that come out stuck to it.

Off-hand, I recall at least one instance where the word भल्लूक' is used to 
mean 'a bear'.  In उत्तररामचरित of भवभूति, RAma is describing the forest as 
he flies over it.  He describes a cave in it as ’दधति कुहरभाजामत्र 
भल्लूकयूनाम्। अनुरसितगुरूणि स्त्यानमम्बूकृतानि। a cave reverberating with 
the अम्बू cries of the cave-dwelling young bears.

As to the second line quoted by you सा हि बिलस्थान्सर्पादिन्श्वासबलेनाकृष्य 
भक्षयतीति प्रसिद्धम्। (सा बिलस्थान् सर्पादीन् श्वासबलेन आकृष्य भक्षयति इति 
प्रसिद्धम्) it looks more like imagination of olden times, uncorroborated 
by hard evidence.   Bears do not eat serpents, much less so by sucking them 
in by inhaling air. There are several such poetic conventions in Sanskrit 
literature.  They should not be understood as literal truths. 

Arvind Kolhatkar.
