In an otherwise admirable effort, I would suggest that the title should be 

correctly written as शुक्लयजुर्वेदी*यं* बृहदार*ण्य*कोपनिषद् and not 

as शुक्लयजुर्वेदी*या* बृहदार*न्य*कोपनिषद् .



This is not to distract from the quality of work that is offered but in the 

spirit of improving upon on it.  The Upanishad's were written by अरण्य 

dwellers and बृहदार*ण्य*कोपनिषद् underscores that fact.

## 5. Arvind_Kolhatkar — 2015-04-13 21:49:27

Thanks to Radim Navyan, this exchange has blown away a cobweb in my head. 

 Following my mother tongue Marathi, which, like all Indian languages, 

freely uses Sanskrit words, I was under the impression that the word 

उपनिषत् is of the neutral gender.  I have now been able to confirm that in 

Sanskrit it is of the feminine gender.



I should have remembered the following verses where उपनिषत् is clearly used 

in the feminine gender -



सर्वोपनिषदो गावो दोग्धा गोपालनन्दन:।

पार्थो वत्स: सुधीर्भोक्ता दुग्धं गीतामृतं महत्॥ (गीतास्तुति)

All UpaniShad's are the cows, the Darling of cowherds (gopala+nandana = 

KrishNa) is drawing their milk, intelligent Paartha (the son of PrithA - 

KuntI = Arjuna) is the calf drinking the milk which is the nectar of gItA.



उपनिषद: परिपीता गीताऽपि च हन्त मतिपथं नीता।

तदपि हा विधुवदना मानससदनान्न मे याति॥ (भामिनीविलास - जगन्नाथ)

All UpaniShad's have been drunk, gItA has been committed to memory.  Alas, 

even then the lady of the moon-like face (vidhu+vadanA) does not go away 

from my mind.



Arvind Kolhatkar.
