## 12. narayan prasad — 2014-04-15 17:02:07

Comment on असन्दिग्धम् regarding a sUtra:



I could not so far trace the authority of the kaarikaa defining the term

sUtra. So far as the paaNini-sUtras are concerned, at many places you get

doubts.  And that is why an interpretation (व्याख्यान) by the learned is

required to clear such doubt, as quoted by the महाभाष्यकार (the Great

Commentator) at more than one place -

व्याख्यानतो हि विशेषप्रतिपत्तिर्नहि सन्देहादलक्षणम् ।

translated by Dr Kielhorn as:



P.I: The precise (meaning of an ambiguous term) is ascertained from

interpretation, for (a rule), even though it contain an ambiguous term,

must nevertheless teach (something definite).



cf: The परिभाषेन्दुशेखर of नागोजिभट्ट, Vol.II, BORI, 1960, p.2.



--- Narayan Prasad

Из *142780149-Linguistic-Tradition-2006-06-02*, Page 8:

The A consists of a little over four-thousand brief, but algebraically

condensed sūtras ‘threads,’ commonly referred to as rules. A rule is 

formulated

to apply. But more importantly, a rule is to be formulated with a minimum of

syllables (svalpākṣaram), is supposed to be unambiguous (asandigdham),

should carry only the essence (sāravat) of what is to be stated, should be 

allencompassing (viśvatomukham), should be unstoppable in its application

(astobham) and should be creatively new (anavadya). These grammatical rules

are generally verb-less statements structured around a subject and its

corresponding predication.
