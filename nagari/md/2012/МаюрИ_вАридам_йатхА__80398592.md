## 2. Arvind_Kolhatkar — 2012-10-16 17:47:17

The mating time of peacocks coincides with the arrival of the monsoon in 
the Indian subcontinent.   Their mating calls, called केका in Sanskrit, are 
heard around this time.  Out of this natural phenomenon has arisen a 
convention of Sanskrit poetics that peacocks eagerly await the arrival of 
the dark monsoon clouds and exhibit joy upon seeing the dark clouds in the 
sky.  (There are several such conventions in Sanskrit poetics and the 
reader is expected to know them when he reads a piece of Sanskrit 
literature.)

 

वारिद = वारि ददाति 'gives water' is an epithet of the cloud.  The peacock 
मयूर is happy when he sees the वारिद.

 

कालिदास has the following verse in पूर्वमेघ -

 

उत्पश्यामि द्रुतमपि सखे मत्प्रियार्थं यियासो: 

कालक्षेपं ककुभसुरभौ पर्वते पर्वते ते ।

शुक्लापाङ्गै: सजलनयनै: स्वागतीकृत्य केका: 

प्रत्युद्यात: कथमपि भवान् गन्तुमाशु व्यवस्येत् ॥२२॥ 

 

(The विरही यक्ष says to the cloud messenger) O friend, although you are 
hurrying to do my bidding, I do anticipate you taking your time upon every 
mountain peak fragranced by the ककुभ flowers.  How can you manage to rush 
along, when you are beseeched with welcoming calls (केका:) by the peacocks 
with tearful eyes?


Arvind Kolhatkar, Toronto, October 16, 2012.
