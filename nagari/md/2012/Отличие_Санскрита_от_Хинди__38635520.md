## 6. Narayan Prasad — 2012-09-01 11:32:22

<<Такое сравнение применимо к санскриту и дравидским языкам?>>



Not at all. The Dravidian languages form a separate family. A lot of Skt 

words, esp. in case of technical terms, have been borrowed by all the 

Indian languages. It is something like borrowing words of Latin and Greek 

origin for the technical terms in Russian.



The languages of the Slavonic group you have mentioned belong to the modern 

period. Similarity of the same type exists among, say,  Hindi, Bengali, 

Assamese, Oriya, Punjabi etc. For example, with Hindi as the base language, 

a grammar of three and half pages that I got written through a friend, was 

enough for me to learn Oriya and study of  Bengali grammar for three days 

enough for learning Bengali. But although I have spent years together in 

learning Sanskrit, the Tamil language appears to me like Chinese in which, 

the meaning of a sentence with fair length is not at all clear, even when 

the meanings of all words in it are known ! For example, please refer to a 

sentence in Chinese quoted by Bernhard Karlgren in his book "The Chinese 

Language" (1949), p.60; Publisher: The Ronald Press Company, New York. The 

word-for-word English equivalent words given in the order of corresponding 

Chinese words look like this:

*******************************

Old man not far 1000 mile wise come also shall have take profit I state ?

********************************

On this, Karlgren remarks:

Every word in this sentence is simple and easy and of very common 

occurrence in the language. One may find them in any dictionary. And still 

the reader is bewildered. What does this sentence mean ?



--- Narayan Prasad

*мне слово “*шея*” знакомо как *ग्रीव (*наша “грива”?).
Непонятно значения для слова *hand, *на хинди *hāth*, на санскрите *हस्त, 
भुज, कर. *“*Кара*”, понятно где, но вот *हस्त *и есть *hand* - рука 
(кисть), а *भुज *будет *arm* - рука (от кисти до плеча)*.*
Интересно слово *blood*, на санскрите даны как *रक्त *и *रुधिर.* *रुधिर *соответствует 
нашему “*руда*” - кровь, в хинди не присутствует.
